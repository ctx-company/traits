Contract (from 0078, binding): **convergence is the risk, not correctness.** N runs racing must all
land eventually. The merge lock already serializes the critical section — a lost race means someone
landed between release and fast-forward, so a retry queues behind the lock again. The proof's job
is to show that queueing-then-landing actually converges.

`concurrent_disjoint_runs_eventually_land` (`proof_merge_on_completion.rs`) starts two uncoordinated
threads and asserts both finish. Nothing establishes that either process ever waited on the other's
merge-lock hold, so the test passes even when the landings never contend — it proves scheduling
luck, not convergence.

## Scope

Test-only, in `modules/cli/tests/proof_merge_on_completion.rs`: make the contention deterministic.
Establish that the second merge is demonstrably queued behind (or contending for) the first's lock
hold BEFORE the first releases — the lock-holder / lock-wait evidence the merge frames already
record is the observable — then release and assert every run lands, with the loser's frames
recording the wait.

## Watch

- Determinism must come from coordination (hold the lock, observe the queued waiter, release), not
  from sleeps tuned to usually win — that is exactly the flake class this proof exists to kill.
- Keep wall time bounded: `merge.retry-backoff-ms` is configurable, so the fixture can use a small
  base while still exercising real backoff.
- Two coordinated processes are sufficient; do not grow a stress test. Starvation at N is the same
  mechanism as at 2 once queueing is actually proven.

## Done when

The convergence proof fails if the second process never waits on the merge lock; both queued
landings complete and the loser's frames record the wait; `cargo test -p ctx-traits-cli --test
proof_merge_on_completion` passes; the per-round gate is green; landed on `main`.
