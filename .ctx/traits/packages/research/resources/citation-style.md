# Citation Style Guide

Use the canonical A-E source ratings in research-standards.md.

## General Principles

1. Cite every factual claim and statistic.
2. Use an accessible URL or DOI whenever one exists.
3. Put inline citations immediately after the supported claim.
4. Include retrieval dates for mutable web sources.
5. Keep one format throughout a deliverable.
6. Cite the original source rather than a secondary summary whenever possible.

## Web Sources

```text
Inline: (Organization, Year, "Section Title")
Full: Organization Name. (Year). "Page or Section Title." Retrieved [Date] from [URL]

Example: The NIH recommends 150 minutes of moderate activity per week (NIH, 2024, "Physical Activity Guidelines").
```

## Academic Papers

```text
Inline: (Author et al., Year, p. XX)
Full: Author, A. A., Author, B. B., & Author, C. C. (Year). Title. Journal, Volume(Issue), pages. DOI

Example: Recent studies show significant improvement in VO2max with HIIT protocols (Smith et al., 2023, p. 145).
```

## Books

```text
Inline: (Author, Year, p. XX)
Full: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of book. Publisher.

Example: Zone 2 training builds aerobic base capacity (Maffetone, 2015, p. 78).
```

## Direct Quotes

```text
"Exact quote from source" (Author, Year, p. XX)

Example: "Mitochondrial biogenesis is primarily stimulated by endurance exercise at moderate intensities" (Jones & Roberts, 2022, p. 67).
```

Use quotes sparingly. Preserve exact wording and identify the page, paragraph, section, or timestamp.

## Government And Official Reports

```text
Inline: (Agency Abbreviation, Year)
Full: Agency Name. (Year). Report Title. Report Number. Retrieved [Date] from [URL]

Example: The FDA approved three AI diagnostic tools in 2024 (FDA, 2024).
```

## Technical Documentation

```text
Inline: (Company or Project, Year, "Documentation Section")
Full: Company or Project. (Year). "Documentation Title." Version. Retrieved [Date] from [URL]

Example: DynamoDB supports up to 40,000 read capacity units per partition (AWS, 2024, "Partition Throughput Limits").
```

Record the product/version and documentation revision when behavior can change.

## Preprints

```text
Inline: (Author et al., Year, preprint)
Full: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title. Repository identifier [category]. URL

Example: Preliminary results suggest quantum advantage for specific optimization problems (Chen et al., 2024, preprint).
```

Label preprints clearly and search for a peer-reviewed successor.

## Multiple Citations

```text
(Author1, Year; Author2, Year; Author3, Year)

Example: Multiple studies confirm this finding (Smith et al., 2023; Johnson & Lee, 2024; Williams, 2024).
```

Use multiple citations only when each source independently supports the claim or when the sentence explicitly contrasts them.

## Secondary Sources

Avoid when possible. If the original cannot be accessed:

```text
Original work (as cited in Secondary, Year)

Example: Krebs cycle efficiency increases with training (Chen, 2020, as cited in Roberts, 2024, p. 45).
```

Cite both sources when possible and state that the original was not independently inspected.

## Online News And Media

```text
Inline: (Publication, Year)
Full: Publication Name. (Year, Month Day). "Article Title." Retrieved [Date] from [URL]

Example: Tech companies are investing heavily in quantum research (New York Times, 2024).
```

Use news for events and attributed statements, not as the sole evidence for technical, scientific, or causal claims.

## Citation Density

- Executive summary: cite each key factual claim.
- Full report: at least one citation per one to two evidence-bearing paragraphs.
- Findings: cite every major point and all specific data.
- Statistics, quotations, and non-obvious comparisons always need direct support.

## Common Mistakes

Do not:

- Use citations without accessible identifiers.
- Cite sources you did not inspect.
- Mix formats in one deliverable.
- Omit retrieval dates for mutable web sources.
- Leave broken links or cite a page that does not support the claim.

Do:

- Verify every URL before final delivery.
- Include page or section locations when available.
- Provide DOI identifiers for papers.
- Keep bibliography entries connected to inline citations and evidence-table source IDs.
